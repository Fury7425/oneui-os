Rules for any new screen

This page is the OneUI OS style and motion guide. Anything new, in this mockup or in the OS itself, follows these rules and the numbers in the sections below.

  1. Colour comes from the OS tokens. The accent only enters through the palette roles. Other colour appears only in app icons, Settings category circles and warnings.
  2. Shape: pills for controls and bars, 28 to 34 px corners for windows, cards and panels, 16 px for small items.
  3. Material: window glass or floating glass; controls inside are flat tints; never a blur inside a blur; never an animated blur radius; full-screen states sit on the pre-blurred wallpaper.
  4. Motion: arrivals ride a spring and bounce a little; exits use the Exit curve, run about half as long and never bounce; what is nearest the trigger moves first; nothing waits for anything else.
  5. Theme-level changes (dark mode, palette, wallpaper) reveal in a circle from what was clicked.
  6. The wallpaper is the ground. Boot ends on it, setup and sign-in sit on its blurred copy, and the desktop arrives by clearing that blur.
  7. Icons: Material Symbols Rounded, filled, for system glyphs; app icons from the one registry; never Samsung's own assets.
  8. Every screen works in light and dark, in every palette, with themed icons on, at ⅕× speed and with reduced motion (fades only).

Curves and springs

Four cubic curves and five springs cover every movement. Springs carry the bounce: anything arriving overshoots a little and settles, anything leaving doesn't. Hover a card to see the curve run. Springs ship as CSS linear() easings generated from a damped spring, so the duration is whatever the spring needs to settle; the speed control above slows everything, including these.

Glass and blur

Two glass materials, flat tints inside them, and a pre-blurred wallpaper for full-screen states. One UI 9 pushes in the same direction: floating panels with more transparency, stronger blur and real shadow depth.

1
2
3
  1. WallpaperStatic image. A copy blurred once at 36 px is kept for the overview, lock screen and login, so those never run a live blur over the whole screen.
  2. Window glassLive blur 40 px, saturation 180%, fill 84% opaque. Windows are read for long stretches, so they stay calm.
  3. Floating glassSame blur, fill 55–62%. Quick panel, search, drawer, calendar, notification pills, taskbar pills and widgets. More of the wallpaper colour comes through.
  4. ControlsFlat tints (ink 6% in light, white 6–8% in dark). Never a second blur inside glass.

Rules

  1. Never animate the blur radius. A panel fades in and its blur fades in with it, because the blur belongs to the panel. Changing the radius per frame forces a full re-blur every frame, and that is the stutter One UI 9 beta testers describe when the quick panel opens.
  2. One live blur per surface. Tiles, rows and sliders inside glass are tints. The only nested blur is the app drawer's bottom band, which blurs the drawer's own scrolling icons under the search pill.
  3. Keep the colour. Saturation 180% lets the wallpaper's tone survive the blur. Testers of One UI 9 complain when blur is so heavy that colours underneath disappear.
  4. Fade the material, not its parent. Fading a container that holds glass breaks the blur of every pill inside it for the length of the fade. Notifications, widgets and taskbar pills fade one by one.
  5. Depth from shadow. A 1 px light inner edge on top, a hairline border and a soft drop shadow; floating surfaces get the larger shadow.

Color palette

    
Same idea as One UI's Wallpaper and style → Color palette. Every OneUI OS app takes its accent from five numbers: a hue, a chroma and three lightness values. Auto, the swatch marked A, follows the wallpaper: change the wallpaper or switch between light and dark, and the palette changes with it in the same reveal. Mono matches the renders. The wallpaper colors are whatever the current wallpaper actually contains, and the six basic colors are fixed. Apply palette to app icons recolors every icon in two tones of the palette. Try it here or in the prototype under Settings → Wallpaper and style.

    

    
Wallpapers

    

    
How Auto reads a wallpaper

    
    
      
  1. Draw the wallpaper into a 64 × 40 canvas, about 2,500 pixels.
    2. 
      
  2. Convert each pixel to OKLCH and drop greys, near-black and near-white (chroma under 0.02, lightness under 0.12 or over 0.97).
    3. 
      
  3. Add each pixel's chroma to one of 36 hue bins, 10° wide, and smooth each bin with half of its neighbours.
    4. 
      
  4. Keep the three strongest bins at least 40° apart. The first one is Auto; all three become the wallpaper colors.
    5. 
      
  5. Chroma is the bin's average × 1.6, kept between 0.06 and 0.16 so the accent is never neon and never grey. A wallpaper with no colour gives Mono.
    6. 
      
  6. Sampling finishes before the reveal starts, so the wallpaper, the palette and the themed icons change in one circle instead of three.
    7.

Power on to desktop

    
Boot, first-run setup, the lock screen and sign-in, the power menu, sleep, restart and power off all run in the prototype. Use the Run buttons under it, the Lock tile and the power button in quick settings, or the power button on the lock screen.

Boot
The laptop's own firmware logo comes first, as it does today. Then the OneUI OS mark scales in and draws its ribbon, three dots breathe while services start, and black lifts off the blurred wallpaper. Restart and power off show the same mark with one word under it.

      
First boot
"Hello" in ten languages, the same words as the Plasma Setup module. Then five steps on one card: Wi-Fi, account, look, Galaxy features, done. The look step applies light or dark and the palette as you pick them. The card resizes to each step instead of jumping, and Back always works.

      
Lock and sign-in
Clock, now playing and notification icons on the sharp wallpaper. Any click or key moves the clock up and brings in the sign-in on the blurred wallpaper. Galaxy Books have a fingerprint reader in the power key, so that option sits under the password field. Login after boot uses the same screen.

      
Power menu
Four round buttons on the blurred wallpaper, middle two first. Power off is the only warning-coloured one. Esc or a click outside cancels and returns to wherever you came from.

      
Sleep and wake
The display fades out; nothing else moves. A dim hint appears after a second. Waking always lands on the lock screen.

      
The desktop arrives
After setup and after every sign-in: the blur clears, windows settle from 103%, widgets slide in from the left and the three taskbar pills rise, on springs, a few tens of milliseconds apart.

    

  


  

    
Choreography

    
Each interaction as a timeline. Bars start where the movement starts and end where it settles; stacked thin bars are staggered items. Press Play to run it in the prototype at the current speed.

    
EnterExitMovePopSpringLinear (opacity)

    

    
Four habits hold everywhere: content nearest the thing you clicked moves first; arrivals ride a spring and bounce a little, exits never do; exits are about half as long as entrances and never stagger; nothing waits for anything else to finish.

  


  

    
App icons in the drawer

    
Every icon comes from one registry and is drawn the same way in the taskbar (38 px), search (44 px), notifications (44 px) and the drawer (48 px). The drawer is where they move most: they ripple out from the Apps button, lift on hover, and the one you click becomes the window.

    

      
      

        
    
          
  • Tile 48 px with a 33% radius. Where the browser or toolkit supports a squircle corner, it uses that with a 44% radius, which matches One UI's icon shape.
    • 
          
  • The glyph sits in the centre 24 px: 2 px round strokes or a solid fill, white on the app colour.
    • 
          
  • One background per app, L 0.55–0.78, chroma 0.13–0.21 in oklch. Calendar, Camera and Clock are white tiles with a 1 px inner hairline; Terminal, Calculator and Wallet get a hairline ring in dark mode so they don't sink into the glass.
    • 
          
  • Shadow 0 3 8 −4 at 35% black, raised to 0 10 18 −8 on hover.
    • 
        

        

      

    

    
Icon sets for the real build

    
    
      
  • Quick panel and status glyphs: Material Symbols Rounded, filled (Apache-2.0). It is the closest open match to One UI 9's filled quick panel glyphs and is what this prototype uses. Export the needed glyphs as SVG into the OneUI OS icon set.
    • 
      
  • App icons: OneUI4-Icons (GPL-3, built on Fluent, light and dark variants) is the only maintained One UI style theme for Linux. Before shipping it, check the coverage and that none of its icons are copied Samsung artwork; keep Papirus as the fallback.
    • 
      
  • Samsung's own One UI icons can't be redistributed, the same rule as the fonts and logos.
    • 
      
  • Themed icons: Apply palette to app icons needs a monochrome glyph per app. Generate a second icon theme from those glyphs whenever the palette changes.
    • 
    

    
Launch: the icon becomes the window

    
The icon's colour plate stretches into the window's frame on the window spring while the glyph grows and fades. The real window fades in at 45%, the plate fades out at 55%. The other drawer icons shrink to 90% as the drawer leaves, the way One UI's home screen icons pull back when an app opens.

    

  


  

    
The new logo

    
The mark is a crop of the wallpaper: the One UI ribbon on a near-black squircle, with the fold line that runs through it. The desktop, first-run setup and the logo now share one image. It replaces oneui-os-logo.svg, which os-release points to.

Why it changed

    
    
      
  • The old mark was a four-point sparkle on a black disc. That sparkle is the Galaxy AI glyph, so the OS logo and the AI app icon looked like the same thing in the drawer.
    • 
      
  • The ribbon is 23% of the tile and nothing competes with it, so it still reads at 16 px in a tray or a boot menu.
    • 
      
  • One warm colour on near-black. The taskbar button crops it to a circle; the monochrome version is the ribbon alone in the current text colour.

Building it on Plasma 6

    
Where each piece lives in OneUI OS. The browser prototype is the reference for timing; QML and KWin need their own tuning against it.

Children use PauseAnimation delays, bottom row first. Turn off the Sliding Popups effect for these so KWin doesn't slide them as well.

        
Blur and contrastKWin Blur and Background Contrast effectsThe Plasma Style's mask-* SVG elements define the blur region; contrast, intensity and saturation come from the style's [ContrastEffect] settings. Blur strength is global, so the window/floating difference comes from fill opacity, as here.

Switch desktopKWin Slide effectWindows slide, wallpaper stays. Match the 420 ms Move curve; the global animation speed scales it.

        
Quick panel glyphsOneUI OS icon setMaterial Symbols Rounded, FILL 1, weight 500, exported to SVG. Off tiles: translucent circle with the glyph in the text colour; on tiles: solid On colour with On text.

        
Color palettekdeglobals accentA Wallpaper and style page writes the accent colour (Plasma 6 can already take it from the wallpaper). Wallpaper palettes come from Material Color Utilities; Kirigami's highlight colour and the OneUI OS plasmoids follow it.

        
Themed app iconsGenerated icon themeOn palette change, write OneUIOS-Themed: each app's monochrome glyph on the palette's tile tone, inheriting the normal theme, and switch to it.

        
App icon themeIcon theme packagingCandidate OneUI4-Icons (GPL-3), pinned to a commit in build.sh, after checking coverage and provenance; Papirus stays the fallback.

        
Auto paletteSmall user servicePlasma 6 can already take the accent from the wallpaper. For the One UI behaviour, a service watches the wallpaper and light/dark settings, runs the steps in the Color palette section, writes the accent and regenerates the themed icons.

        
Boot, restart, power offPlymouth themeTwo-step plugin: the firmware logo first (as Fedora's bgrt theme does), then the mark and ribbon drawing as animation frames and the three dots as the throbber. The same theme shows "Restarting" and "Shutting down".

        
First bootPlasma Setup modulesThe hello and Galaxy features modules exist; add a look module (light/dark and palette). Fedora's Wi-Fi and user modules pick up the OneUI OS Plasma Style and colours.

        
Lock and sign-inLook-and-feel lockscreen + login greeterOne QML layout for both: the lock screen in the look-and-feel package (KScreenLocker) and the same design as the Plasma Login Manager greeter theme. Fingerprint through fprintd, if libfprint supports the Galaxy Book's power-key sensor.

        
The desktop arrivesLook-and-feel splashThe splash shows the blurred wallpaper and fades out over the desktop. The staggered pills and widgets need plasmashell support; until then it is a plain fade.

        
Power menuLook-and-feel logoutThe logout screen QML: four round buttons on the blurred wallpaper. Sleep goes through systemd, and the screen locker is already up on resume.

        
Dark modeColour scheme changePlasma has no hook to snapshot the screen before a scheme switch, so the circular reveal would need a C++ KWin effect. Until then the switch is instant.
